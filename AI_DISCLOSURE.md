# AI and Resource Disclosure

## AI tools used

- **Claude (Anthropic), an AI assistant**, was used in two ways:
  1. To help me turn my idea into a detailed, staged build prompt (`FOCUS_GARDEN_BUILD_PROMPT.md`, kept outside this repository).
  2. To generate the code stage by stage, explain each new JavaScript concept, and suggest fixes when a test did not pass.

## What was AI-generated

All code files were generated by the AI assistant from my specifications, one stage at a time:
`manifest.json`, `background.js`, `shared/rules.js`, `shared/plant.js`, `shared/variation.js`, and everything in `pages/`. It also drafted this repository's `README.md`, `TALKING_POINTS.md`, and this file, which I reviewed and edited.

## Decisions that were mine

- The core concept: an allowlist for the task, where time on it grows the plant and time off it leaves marks the plant grows past.
- The "Working in another app" declaration, with deliberate friction (app name plus at least three words of purpose, 25-minute expiry).
- The non-punitive framing: detours, never failures, penalties, or dead plants; the gentle welcome-back message.
- Designing for students with ADHD as the target audience.
- Making the gallery and seeded variation required features, and asking for varied greens as well as flower colors.
- Demo mode at 10x speed, and the 15-minute minimum session.
- The plant rules (growth rates, leaf and flower timing, kink angles, knot sizes), the 10-second grace period, and the four states.
- During the build: treating `youtube.com` and `www.youtube.com` as the same site, and softening the detour knot so it reads as a growth node instead of a dark lump.

Some smaller implementation choices were suggested by the AI and accepted by me, for example: neutral moments do not break an unbroken focus stretch, each detour segment counts as one detour (so the count matches the knots on the plant), and splitting `summary.js` and `variation.js` into their own files to keep every file under 300 lines.

## How I verified the code

Every stage was tested by me in Chrome before it was committed, using a written test checklist with an expected result for each step (for the tracking engine: a debug page showing each recorded segment). When something did not work, I reported the exact behavior or error, and the fix was tested again before committing. The commit history shows the stages in order.
